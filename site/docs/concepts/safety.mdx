---
title: Safety numbers
description: A fingerprint two people compare to find out nobody is in between.
---

Encryption protects a conversation from everyone except whoever you're
actually talking to. A safety number is how two people check that the person
on the other end is the one they think.

## What it is

```swift
let generator = NumericFingerprintGenerator(iterations: 5200)
let fingerprint = try generator.create(
    version: 2,
    localIdentifier: ourId, localKey: ourIdentityKey,
    remoteIdentifier: theirId, remoteKey: theirIdentityKey
)

let digits = try fingerprint.displayable.formatted
let scannable = try fingerprint.scannable.serialize()
```


An iterated hash over both identity keys and both identifiers, which gives the
same number to both sides and a different one to anybody else.

It comes in two forms. A sixty-digit display form people read out or compare
on screen, and a scannable form that goes behind a QR code so one phone can
check another.

The hash is iterated thousands of times. Finding a collision costs an attacker
enormously more than comparing costs the two people doing it.

## When it changes

The number changes when an identity key changes. That happens for ordinary
reasons, like a reinstall on a new device, and for one bad reason.

Your app can't tell those apart, and neither can gossveil. What it can do is stop and
say so, instead of carrying on with a key nobody checked. That decision lives
in your identity store: it gets asked whether an identity is trusted, and the
answer is yours.

## Comparing

```swift
let same = try fingerprint.scannable.compare(againstEncoding: theirs)
```


The scannable form compares as bytes, which either match or don't. The display
form is for humans, and the point of sixty digits is that reading them aloud
is tedious enough to be done carefully and short enough to be done at all.
